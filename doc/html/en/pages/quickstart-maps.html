Turning on maps#

This page shows how to get your first maps into QMapShack and how to show them.

Images: © OpenStreetMap contributors; OpenTopoMap, CC-BY-SA; trails by waymarkedtrails.org.

Getting the built-in maps#

QMapShack comes with five online maps, among them OpenStreetMap and OSM Topo. An online map needs an internet connection: QMapShack loads the map while you look at it.

  1. Click File → Setup Map Paths. The same entry is on the menu when you right-click the list in the Maps panel.

  2. Click Help! I want maps! I don’t want to read the documentation!

  3. QMapShack asks Where do you want to store maps? and already offers the Maps folder of your home folder. Confirm it. The Setup Map Paths dialog then closes by itself.

  4. The Maps panel lists the maps. A map is gray while it is off.

    QMapShack names each map after its file, without the file type and with underscores as spaces: it lists OSM_Topo.tms as OSM Topo. It names elevation data and POI files the same way.

Turning on a map#

  1. To turn a map on, click the button in its row in the Maps panel. Click it again to turn the map off.

  2. Turn on OpenStreetMap. Its row turns bold and says active, and the map replaces the welcome page.

Stacking maps#

When several maps are on, they lie on top of each other in the order of the list: a map covers the maps above it.

  1. Turn on OSM Topo as well. It is below OpenStreetMap in the list, so OSM Topo covers OpenStreetMap.

  2. To change the order, drag a map up or down in the list. Or select it and press Shift+Up or Shift+Down; the right-click menu has Move Up and Move Down as well. With OSM Topo moved up, OpenStreetMap covers it.

Making a map see-through#

  1. To see the lower map through the upper one, click the arrow to the left of the upper map’s name. Its settings open below the row.

  2. Drag the slider under the name to the left to lower the map’s opacity. Here OSM Topo is half see-through over OpenStreetMap.
